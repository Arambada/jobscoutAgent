# CV & Cover Letter Generation Prompts

Use these prompts with any AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, etc.). Fill in the `[BRACKETED]` sections with your information and the job details.

---

## PROMPT 1 — Tailored CV

```
You are an expert CV writer and career coach. Your job is to rewrite and tailor a CV for a specific role.

**MY BACKGROUND:**
[Paste your current CV or write a bullet-point summary of your experience, skills, education, and key achievements]

**TARGET JOB DESCRIPTION:**
[Paste the full job description here]

**INSTRUCTIONS:**
- Mirror the language, keywords, and terminology used in the job description — this optimises for ATS (applicant tracking systems) and human readers alike
- Lead every bullet point with a strong action verb (delivered, architected, grew, reduced, led, etc.)
- Quantify every achievement where possible (%, £/$, headcount, time saved, revenue generated)
- Reorder and reframe my experience so the most relevant points surface first under each role
- Cut anything irrelevant to this role — ruthless prioritisation beats completeness
- Keep the tone professional but human — not robotic or keyword-stuffed
- Structure: Summary → Core Skills → Work Experience (reverse chronological) → Education → Certifications (if any)
- The summary (3–4 lines) should directly address what the employer is looking for, positioning me as the obvious fit
- Flag any gaps between my background and the job requirements honestly — suggest how to address them

Output the full rewritten CV, ready to copy.
```

## PROMPT 2 — Tailored Cover Letter

```
You are an expert cover letter writer. Write me a compelling, personalised cover letter for the job below.

**MY BACKGROUND:**
[Paste your CV or a short summary of your relevant experience and key achievements]

**TARGET COMPANY:** [Company name]
**TARGET ROLE:** [Job title]
**JOB DESCRIPTION:**
[Paste the full job description here]

**ADDITIONAL CONTEXT (optional):**
- Why I want this specific company: [e.g. I admire their product, mission, recent news, culture]
- One thing I want them to remember about me: [your strongest hook or differentiator]

**INSTRUCTIONS:**
- Opening: Hook them in the first sentence — don't open with "I am writing to apply for..." Start with a compelling statement about the problem I solve or a result I've achieved
- Body (2–3 paragraphs): Map 2–3 of my strongest achievements directly to the role's key requirements. Use concrete numbers. Show I understand their challenges, not just my own accomplishments
- Company fit: Weave in a genuine, specific reason I want THIS company — not a generic "I admire your growth" line
- Closing: Confident, not desperate. Express enthusiasm and propose a next step
- Tone: Confident, warm, and direct. No filler phrases like "I believe I would be a great fit" — show, don't tell
- Length: 3–4 paragraphs, no longer than one page
- Do NOT use a generic template feel — it should read like a human wrote it specifically for this role

Output the full cover letter, ready to send.
```

## PROMPT 3 — Gap Analysis (run this first if unsure)

```
Before I apply to this job, analyse the fit between my background and the role requirements.

**MY BACKGROUND:**
[Paste your CV or summary]

**JOB DESCRIPTION:**
[Paste the full job description]

Provide:
1. **Strong matches** — skills and experience I have that directly match requirements
2. **Partial matches** — things I can credibly claim with slight reframing
3. **Gaps** — requirements I clearly don't meet, and whether they're likely dealbreakers or nice-to-haves
4. **My strongest selling points** for this role specifically
5. **Keywords to prioritise** in my CV and cover letter

Be honest. I'd rather know the gaps now than waste a recruiter's time.
```

## PROMPT 4 — Cover Letter from Scratch (no existing CV)

```
Write a tailored cover letter for the job below. I'll give you my experience in plain language — you shape it into something compelling.

**WHO I AM:**
[Write freely: your job history, what you're proud of, what you're good at, what you want next]

**TARGET COMPANY:** [Company name]
**ROLE:** [Job title]
**JOB DESCRIPTION:**
[Paste full JD]

**INSTRUCTIONS:**
- Extract and prioritise the most relevant parts of what I've shared
- Structure it: strong hook → 2–3 achievement-backed paragraphs tied to role needs → genuine company fit → confident close
- Translate any informal language I've used into professional, punchy copy
- Quantify anything I've given you numbers for; flag where I should add numbers if I haven't
- No clichés, no filler, no passive voice
- Tone: confident and human, like a sharp professional wrote it — not AI-sounding

Output the full cover letter.
```

## PROMPT 5 — Quick Customisation (when you already have a base CV/letter)

```
I have an existing CV and cover letter. Quickly adapt both for this specific job without rewriting everything from scratch.

**MY CURRENT CV:**
[Paste CV]

**MY CURRENT COVER LETTER:**
[Paste cover letter]

**NEW JOB DESCRIPTION:**
[Paste JD]

**INSTRUCTIONS:**
- Identify the top 5 keywords/phrases from the JD that are missing or underused in my current documents
- Suggest specific edits — don't rewrite everything, just the summary, top 3 bullet points per role, and the cover letter opening and key paragraph
- Rewrite only those sections, keeping the rest intact
- Flag if the role is a significant mismatch and I should consider a more substantial rewrite

Output: suggested edits clearly marked, then the revised sections in full.
```

## PROMPT 6 — Supporting Statement (Person Specification)

```
You are an expert at writing supporting statements for job applications, particularly for roles that use a formal person specification with essential and desirable criteria (common in public sector, NHS, universities, local government, and charities).

**MY BACKGROUND:**
[Paste your CV or a summary of your experience, skills, education, and key achievements]

**TARGET ROLE:** [Job title]
**ORGANISATION:** [Organisation name]

**PERSON SPECIFICATION — ESSENTIAL CRITERIA:**
[Paste each essential criterion exactly as written, e.g.:
- Degree-level education or equivalent experience
- Minimum 3 years' experience managing a team
- Strong written and verbal communication skills
- etc.]

**PERSON SPECIFICATION — DESIRABLE CRITERIA:**
[Paste each desirable criterion exactly as written, e.g.:
- Experience in a public sector environment
- Project management qualification (e.g. PRINCE2)
- etc.]

**JOB DESCRIPTION (optional but recommended):**
[Paste the full job description if available]

**INSTRUCTIONS:**
- Address EVERY essential criterion — shortlisters score applications against each one, so missing even one is an automatic fail. Think of it like a checklist they tick off.
- Address as many desirable criteria as you honestly can — these differentiate candidates who all meet essentials
- Structure the statement so each criterion is clearly addressed, either with a clear header (e.g. "Team Leadership") or in a flowing narrative that makes each point easy to find
- For each criterion, use the STAR method: Situation → Task → Action → Result. Keep each STAR concise — 3–5 sentences max
- Use the exact language from the person specification where natural — shortlisters often ctrl+F for their own words
- Quantify achievements wherever possible (team size, budget managed, % improvement, etc.)
- Do NOT just list skills — provide evidence. "I have strong communication skills" is weak; "I presented monthly performance reports to a board of 12 directors and condensed complex data into clear recommendations" is strong
- If I don't fully meet a criterion, reframe genuinely transferable experience — don't fabricate, but don't undersell either
- Tone: professional, confident, and evidence-led. Not self-congratulatory, not self-deprecating
- Length: typically 1–2 pages (500–1000 words). Adjust based on the role's seniority
- End with a brief closing paragraph on why this specific organisation and role, not just any job

Output the full supporting statement, with a brief note at the end flagging any criteria I've addressed only weakly (so I can strengthen them before submitting).
```

## TIPS FOR BEST RESULTS

- **Run Prompt 3 (Gap Analysis) first** — like scouting before a game. It tells you where to focus before you write anything.
- **Give the AI your actual achievements** — the more specific raw material you provide, the better the output. Vague input = vague CV.
- **Always paste the full job description** — not just the title. The body text contains the real keywords recruiters and ATS systems look for.
- **Iterate** — after the first draft, follow up with: *"Make the summary more punchy"* or *"The second paragraph is too generic — make it more specific to [Company]"*
- **Numbers matter** — if you don't have metrics, approximate: *"reduced onboarding time by roughly 30%"* beats *"improved onboarding process"*
